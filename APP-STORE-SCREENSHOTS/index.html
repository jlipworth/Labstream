    App Store screenshot automation¶

    scripts/app-store-screenshots.py has two capture modes that share one export format and validator:

    • Fixture mode (capture) drives the real native shells with the Debug-only synthetic browse catalog and launch routes. It does not authenticate, contact a media server, or read persisted accounts. Its images are layout evidence only: every manifest says so, and none may be uploaded.
    • Live-catalog mode (live start|shot|finish) captures an ordinary signed-in session against the controlled licensed review catalog. The exporter launches the installed app with no arguments at all, a person signs in and navigates normally, and each shot requires the same process to still be running. This is the only lane that can produce final images; see the licensed-media capture plan.

    The licensed catalog's public source and license provenance is checked in at scripts/app-review-media-provenance.json and covered by scripts/tests/test_app_store_screenshots.py. Raw runner evidence stays under the ignored artifacts/ tree; an export contains only JPEGs and a sanitized checksum manifest.

    Apple requirements checked on 2026-11-05¶

    Source: Apple's screenshot specifications, upload requirements, and the linked iPhone Duo launch page. Apple allows one to ten PNG, JPEG, or JPG screenshots per device size and forbids alpha channels and transparency. Apple can change classes and sizes independently of this repository, so recheck before each release.

    Apple's page now names iPhone classes rather than inch sizes. Its "Required device sizes" section reads: "iPhone: At least one screenshot for iPhone with Dynamic Island (medium display)" and "iPad (if your app supports iPadOS): At least one screenshot for iPad 13-inch display." Mac, Apple TV, and Apple Vision Pro screenshots are each required for apps on that platform.

    Apple display class (repository shorthand) Accepted portrait / landscape pixels Apple's requirement text Exporter target
    Apple Vision Pro 3840 × 2160 Required for Apple Vision Pro visionos
    iPhone Duo, outer display 1398 × 2034 / 2034 × 1398 Starting in April 2027, required for any app using the iOS 27.1 SDK or later; submitting Duo screenshots adds a "made for iPhone Duo" product-page badge duo-outer
    iPhone Duo, inner display 2007 × 2853 / 2853 × 2007 Same as outer duo-inner
    iPhone with Dynamic Island, large ("6.9-inch") 1260 × 2736, 1290 × 2796, 1320 × 2868 (and landscape) None stated; falls back to scaled Face ID (large) iphone69 (1320 × 2868)
    iPhone with Face ID, large ("6.5-inch") 1284 × 2778, 1242 × 2688 (and landscape) Required if the app runs on iPhone and Dynamic Island (large) screenshots aren't provided none (fallback class)
    iPhone with Dynamic Island, medium ("6.3-inch") 1179 × 2556, 1206 × 2622 (and landscape) Required (see above) iphone (1206 × 2622)
    iPad 13-inch 2064 × 2752, 2048 × 2732 (and landscape) Required if the app runs on iPad ipad (2064 × 2752)
    Apple TV 1920 × 1080 or 3840 × 2160 Required for Apple TV apps tvos (3840 × 2160)
    Mac 1280 × 800, 1440 × 900, 2560 × 1600, 2880 × 1800 Required for Mac apps macos (2560 × 1600)

    Smaller iPhone and iPad classes are optional and scaled from these. Labstream builds with the iOS 27.1 SDK, so the Duo requirement applies to its submissions from April 2027; a 1.8.0 submission before then may omit Duo screenshots but loses the badge.

    What Apple's page does not state (recorded as unknown)¶

    • No inch sizes: "6.9-, 6.5-, 6.3-inch" are repository shorthand for the named classes.
    • It does not reconcile its two iPhone rules: Dynamic Island (medium) is listed as required, while Face ID (large) is required unless Dynamic Island (large) is provided. Coverage enforces both, so the conservative iPhone set is 6.3-inch and 6.9-inch (or 6.3-inch and the 6.5-inch fallback).
    • It gives only "April 2027" for Duo; coverage treats 2027-04-01 as the effective date.
    • It does not say whether both Duo displays are needed or whether either one suffices, or whether Duo screenshots scale to/from other iPhone classes. Coverage requires both Duo displays.

    These rules and unknowns are machine-readable in scripts/app-store-screenshot-specs.json (displayClasses, coverageRules, unknowns, verifiedAt).

    Scenes and fixture routes¶

    Each target captures named scenes, exported as <target>-<NN>-<scene>-<W>x<H>.jpg so the number gives the upload order. Fixture mode reaches each scene through Debug-only launch routes, so no simulator taps are needed:

    Scene Fixture route Targets
    home --ui-testing-fixture browse all
    library --ui-testing-open-first-library all except Mac (its sidebar lists libraries from the live catalog repository, which has no fixture data)
    detail --ui-testing-open-item plex-orbit all
    playback --ui-testing-fixture player --ui-testing-player-paused (Mac unpaused; Duo inner adds --ui-testing-player-present-delay 8 so the appResize session starts first) iPhone, iPad, Duo, tvOS, Mac; visionOS has no fixture route (spatial player, passive-only simulator)
    downloads --ui-testing-initial-tab offline all except tvOS (streaming-only, no Downloads)

    The tab, first-library, and open-item routes are applied for every product by RootView (DebugUITestLaunchConfiguration.applySharedCaptureRoute); Settings routing stays in the mobile shell. The platform runners pass routes through --launch-arg, which accepts only --ui-testing-* flags and plain values.

    Mobile playback locks to landscape, so those images are landscape sizes of the same class. The Duo inner target is a proxy: a devicectl device appResize session at 669 × 951 points (3×, Apple's 2007 × 2853 inner size) on the folded simulator, cropped from the Resizable display. Simulator folding is GUI-only and simctl io screenConfig power changes do not unfold the device, so the proxy proves layout only. Live Duo inner captures read the real inner display (primary-1) after a person unfolds the simulator in Device Hub; live outer captures read primary.

    Fixture capture¶

    Hold the repository's one-simulator lease first. No other simulator may be booted. The exporter resolves only IDs recorded by scripts/worktree-sim.sh, captures one target at a time (building once per target and keeping the simulator booted between its scenes), and shuts each simulator down before the next target. The 6.9-inch lane uses its own Pro Max-only simulator (scripts/worktree-sim.sh --platform iphone69 setup); the default iPhone simulator is unchanged.

    scripts/app-store-screenshots.py capture --target all --allow-simulator
scripts/app-store-screenshots.py capture --target iphone69 --target duo-outer \
  --scene home --scene detail --allow-simulator
scripts/app-store-screenshots.py capture --target macos

    Targets: visionos, iphone, iphone69, ipad, duo-outer, duo-inner, tvos, macos. The command prints the export directory, artifacts/app-store-screenshots/<UTC>-fixture/export. Scenes without a fixture route are listed in the manifest's skippedScenes.

    Capture refuses an uncommitted source tree so its manifest commit is authoritative. The explicit --allow-dirty escape hatch is for local layout review only; its manifest records gitDirty: true.

    Live-catalog capture¶

    Live mode never passes launch arguments and never accepts credentials. Install the release candidate on the target's worktree simulator first, then for each target:

    scripts/app-store-screenshots.py live start --target iphone69 --allow-simulator
# A person signs in to the review server through the normal Jellyfin login, then navigates.
scripts/app-store-screenshots.py live shot <session> --scene home
scripts/app-store-screenshots.py live shot <session> --scene detail
scripts/app-store-screenshots.py live finish <session>

    live start refuses to boot while another simulator is booted, overrides the iPhone/iPad status bar to 9:41 with full signal, launches with no arguments, and records the process ID plus the installed marketing/build version. live shot refuses a relaunched process, waits for two identical frames, checks the display-class size, and replaces any earlier shot of the same scene. live finish clears the status bar and shuts the simulator down. A session's export lives at <session>/export. macOS has no live lane yet; capture its window by hand with the isolated host path and apply the same checks.

    Automated sign-in and navigation (live drive)¶

    live drive replaces the person in the steps above with the opt-in live-catalog UI test for the session's product (LabstreamMobileLiveCatalogUITests, LabstreamTVLiveCatalogUITests, or LabstreamVisionLiveCatalogUITests). The test attaches to the process that live start launched without arguments (XCUIApplication.activate(); the session's PID check still applies). If the app is signed out, the test signs in with username and password. It then goes Home → Movies → Spring details → play, and the driver runs live shot at the home, library, detail and playback checkpoints.

    cp scripts/app-review-live.env.example scripts/app-review-live.env   # gitignored; fill in, chmod 600
# Build the target's UI tests once (iPhone/iPad shown; tvOS: LabstreamTV + LabstreamTVUITests plan;
# visionOS: Labstream scheme).
scripts/xcodebuild-versioned.sh -project Labstream.xcodeproj -scheme LabstreamMobile \
  -testPlan LabstreamTests -destination "platform=iOS Simulator,id=$SIMID" \
  -derivedDataPath build/DerivedData-ios-iphone build-for-testing CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO
xcrun simctl install "$SIMID" build/DerivedData-ios-iphone/Build/Products/Debug-iphonesimulator/Labstream.app
SESSION=$(scripts/app-store-screenshots.py live start --target iphone --allow-simulator --dry-run \
  | sed -n 's/^SESSION: //p')
scripts/app-store-screenshots.py live drive "$SESSION" \
  --xctestrun build/DerivedData-ios-iphone/Build/Products/*.xctestrun
scripts/app-store-screenshots.py live finish "$SESSION"

    The server address, username and password come only from scripts/app-review-live.env or the caller's environment (LABSTREAM_LIVE_AUTH_SERVER, LABSTREAM_LIVE_AUTH_USERNAME, LABSTREAM_LIVE_AUTH_PASSWORD). They reach the test as xcodebuild TEST_RUNNER_ variables and are never printed. Missing values stop the command as blocked before anything runs, and the tests themselves skip. XCUITest logs typed text, so the driver keeps only a redacted <session>/private-test.log and deletes the result bundle (--keep-result keeps it for debugging; it contains the typed values). The test never signs out. An app signed in to another account fails at the catalog check, so sign out by hand first.

    --dry-run on live start adds "dryRun": true and a note to the export manifest. Dry-run exports prove that the automation works and are never final screenshots: coverage --live-only rejects them. Final screenshots come from a later live session without --dry-run, reviewed by the release owner.

    Validate and check coverage¶

    scripts/app-store-screenshots.py validate <export>
scripts/app-store-screenshots.py coverage <export> [<export> ...] [--live-only] \
  [--target ipad ...] [--as-of 2027-04-01] [--include-upcoming]

    validate fails unless every image has an accepted pixel size for its target's display class, no alpha channel, the recorded SHA-256, at most ten images per class, and the wording that matches its mode: fixture entries must record their --ui-testing flags and the fixture content line; live entries must record an empty argument list and the live-catalog content line. No manifest may claim releaseEligible: true; release approval is a human decision outside the tool.

    coverage validates each export, unions their display classes, and fails on any missing coverageRules entry. Requirements that are not yet in effect (Duo before April 2027) are reported as upcoming and fail only with --include-upcoming. Use --live-only for a release set so a fixture export can never satisfy coverage.

    Determinism and privacy boundary¶

    Fixture captures use --ui-testing --ui-testing-backend plex --ui-testing-fixture browse|player plus routes. The fixture contains invented titles, people, studio names, summaries, IDs, and a non-resolving fixture.invalid URL. Artwork requests cannot reach a real service, so the production UI renders its own placeholders. Mobile captures override the status bar to 9:41 with full Wi-Fi and battery and clear it during cleanup. Release builds do not compile the catalog or routes.

    Exports exclude runner logs, video, .xcresult data, app paths, simulator IDs, host paths, server addresses, accounts, and launch manifests. Keep the generated artifacts/ tree untracked.

    Automated versus gated¶

    Automated: clean builds, exact-product installation, fixture launch per scene, process/log smoke checks supplied by the platform runners, device/window capture, Duo display selection, conversion, dimensions, alpha rejection, checksums, mode wording, and display-class coverage.

    Outside automation:

    • Apple Vision Pro interaction and playback: live drive signs in and navigates through XCUITest element queries, but nothing in automation proves gaze, pinch-drag, immersive, or headset rendering. Coordinate clicking must not be used.
    • iPhone Duo folding: the inner fixture image is an appResize proxy; a real unfold (Device Hub or hardware) is required for final inner images.
    • Mac host permissions: the isolated Accessibility driver and window-only capture require the host permissions in macOS development. A missing permission is a blocker, not a reason to capture the full desktop.
    • Hardware truth: simulator images do not prove HDR, HDMI/audio, Siri Remote, gaze, comfort, performance, or physical-device rendering.
    • Editorial and legal review: a person must inspect every final image for composition, clipped content, transient system UI, branding, licensed artwork, credits, and privacy.
    • Upload: App Store Connect authentication, localization, ordering, and upload remain a release-owner action. The workflow never accepts App Store credentials.
